HaptiWind

Wind-based haptic feedback for remote communication.

#RemoteHaptics#InteractiveSystem#GestureDrivenMovement#TactileCommunication

As an international student, I can only reach the people I love through a screen during important moments. Video and voice keep us in touch, but they lack the warmth of real presence.

HaptiWind adds a third sense to a video call. A gesture sensor reads one person’s hand, and a motorized air duct on the other side moves and shapes a stream of air to match it, so a high-five or a gentle touch arrives as wind on the skin.

Research

Can the brain be tricked into feeling touch?

We perceive presence through multisensory integration: vision, hearing and touch combine into one sense of “being there.” Remote calls deliver only two of the three.

Research on sensory substitution and mirror-touch synesthesia shows that touch can be re-mapped. Force Image Schema theory adds that the brain reads force through familiar patterns of speed, direction, pressure and temperature. If wind reproduces those cues, it can read as touch.

Compared with solid (vibration motors) and liquid media, air is easy to install, common in daily life, contact-free, and can vary in temperature, direction, force and rhythm.

System

Sense, interpret, transmit, activate

Hand gestures are captured in real time, turned into control signals and sent to a remote receiver, which generates matching airflow patterns. Each gesture is mapped to a wind profile.

Pinch · light, brief warm pulsesPunch · strong bursts of warm airHigh five · quick, sharp bursts
Sense

Leap Motion reads the hand position as tx, ty, tz.

Interpret

TouchDesigner maps tx and tz to the machine range and the gesture to a PWM range.

Transmit

A Serial DAT in TouchDesigner sends a command string to the Arduino.

Activate

The Arduino parses X, Z and A, drives the stepper motors and sets the air pump.

Iteration

From fixed outlets to a moving duct

The first version used a fan with swappable outlets. Mapping was clear, but fixed outlets could not deliver direction. The second version put the air pump on a two-axis stepper rig so the airflow follows the hand.

Plan A

Valve and outlet control

  • Clear gesture-to-airflow mapping
  • Limited realism due to fixed outlets
  • Hard to deliver directional or immersive feedback
Plan B · final

Movable air duct control

  • More natural and dynamic touch simulation
  • Better spatial accuracy and realism
  • Slightly more complex, but worth it for the experience
Calm·Smart wearable

CoolSense

A personal device for self-emotional awareness and regulation.

#SmartWearable#EmotionalRegulation#GSRSensor#StressManagement

Public speaking, presentations and academic defenses make stress visible: flushing, sweating, a shaky voice. Once people notice, the anxiety gets worse.

CoolSense is a collar that reads skin conductance through a GSR sensor. When arousal passes a set threshold, it cools the carotid area at the neck, helping the wearer return to a focused state without numbing them.

Hover to focus · click to enlarge

Theory

Stay at the peak, not below it

The Yerkes–Dodson law describes performance as a curve over arousal. Too little and we are disengaged; too much and executive function and verbal fluency break down. Real-time physiological intervention can flatten the curve back toward focus.

Zone 1Calm, relaxed
Zone 2 · peakEngaged, focused
Zone 3Frustrated, anxious
Zone 4Overwhelmed
CoolSense is not a relaxation device that constantly suppresses emotion. It intervenes only when the wearer leaves the optimal zone.

Why these choices

GSR to sense, the carotid to cool

HRV, EMG, EEG and facial recognition were all considered. EEG and face tracking offer rich data but need complex equipment or raise privacy concerns. GSR balances sensitivity, portability and real-time response.

Cooling the carotid area helps regulate the autonomic nervous system and prevents visible responses like facial flushing.

Dream·Wearable + mobile app

LucidLoop

Redefining the dream experience.

#EEG#SleepTech#GenerativeAI#ServiceDesign

LucidLoop is a dream visualization and journaling system. An optional EEG headband monitors sleep; on waking, users voice-record what they remember and receive AI-generated visual recaps.

Instead of artificially inducing dreams, it protects natural dream continuity, with pre-sleep reinforcement that helps people re-enter or extend a dream narrative.

Survey · 122 participants

Most people dream often and forget almost all of it

A survey on dream habits, lucid awareness and attitudes toward recording dreams. Most respondents were 18–34, with 43.4% aged 18–24.

What users asked for

“I want to remix past dreams or link them into a storyline.” “Let it generate visuals that really match my dream, not generic animations.” “I’d love to tag dreams with emotions or themes and track them over time.” “Remind me gently before sleep about what I wanted to dream.”

System flow

Before, during and after sleep

The app and headband share the work across the night. Training follows established techniques such as MILD (wake, recall, set intention, return to sleep) and WBTB (wake back to bed).

Before sleep

Set up training

Onboarding, choose a lucid training plan and goal. The app suggests a path and sends a recap of last night’s dream as a nudge.

During sleep

Monitor quietly

The headband reads EEG to detect REM windows and lucid indicators, and logs training effectiveness and sleep quality.

After sleep

Capture and continue

Voice-record the dream. AI transcribes and analyzes it, generates a visual recap, and helps continue the storyline.

Design

A soft headband and a dreamlike app

The headband is lightweight and skin-friendly (soft elastic, silicone gel) and shaped like a hairband so it is easy to take off. The app combines training, dream logging, AI visualization and sleep summaries.

Life’s End

An AI-powered board game of hidden endings and shifting values.

#AIGameplay#HiddenEndings#SocialInfluence#ResourceExchange#ReplayableLifePaths

Recommendations, ads, feeds and group pressure shape how we see our options. I once had no interest in new running shoes. After enough reviews and “super comfortable” comments, I went from “not interested” to “maybe they’re good” to adding them to my cart.

Life’s End turns that drift into play. Players move through four life stages, trading resources and uncovering private clues, while an AI console pushes events and rumors. No two endings are the same.

YouthAdulthoodMidlifeOld Age

Research

Values are shaped by limited information

Incomplete, biased and fragmented information leads to group polarization, value distortion and one-sided interpretation, which in turn change how people vote, spend and form social attitudes.

Players should feel the tension between public opinion and personal judgment, and notice how their stance was shaped.

References: Buckminster Fuller’s World Game, and the hidden-information mechanics of The Resistance and Avalon, shifted toward life values and path selection.

Game setting

One AI console, private phones

The desktop AI host distributes cards, generates events, tracks resources and adjusts the storyline. Each player’s phone shows their identity, goal and private hints, which keeps information asymmetric. A few physical markers and task boards keep the tabletop feel, and the host archives every decision for replay.

“Someone is secretly accumulating wealth.” Public events are rumors with no confirmed source. They shape how the group perceives and trusts each other.
